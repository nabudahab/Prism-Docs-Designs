What the unified designs keep

Both unified designs were built from the same five choices:

  1. The Datasheet sidebar; Datasheet's address-space figure moves to the address map page.
  2. The Exploded view landing animation.
  3. Coursebook's typography, its search in the sidebar and its previous and next progression, without any record of what has been read.
  4. The current site's beam through the prism, moved off the landing page.
  5. Breadcrumbs and implementation-status labels.

Both place the beam on the Architecture overview, where it now does a job: each ray is a button that shows its layer or contract in the interactive block diagram on the same page. An About page was considered and set aside, because the site has no About content for it to hold yet.

Unified A (Claude)

A reference manual with a reading order. A graphite title bar and contents tree, a white sheet, and the four contract colours as the only colour. The landing animation sits on a dark band.

Open Unified A
Layout
Title bar with the breadcrumbs, contents tree with search pinned at its head, the page, and an outline rail on wide screens.
Navigation
The tree is in reading order: Start, Tutorials, Architecture, Guides, Reference, Tools, Project. Previous and next follow it exactly. On a phone, a bar at the bottom holds previous, the contents and next, with each destination's name.
Type
Charis SIL for headings and prose; Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for navigation, tables and labels; Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono for addresses and code.
Technical content
The address-space figure is a fold on the address map, open on the overview and closed when a link names a region; the region in view is marked. Status labels appear in the tree and after the breadcrumb.

Strengths

  • The landing animation keeps the presence it had in Exploded view.
  • One order everywhere: what the tree lists next is what Next opens.
  • The phone reading bar keeps previous and next in reach and names both.
  • Highest body-text contrast of the two, 17.7 : 1.

Weaknesses

  • The reading-order tree puts Reference fifth. A returning engineer reaches it by search or by scrolling the tree.
  • The dark band and the dark title bar are a stronger contrast with the white pages than Unified B's single surface.
  • The phone reading bar takes a strip at the bottom of the screen.
  • The landing headline runs to five lines at 1440 pixels.

Unified B (GPT-6.1 Sol)

An engineering manual with interactive figures, on one quiet surface. Warm white paper, slate ink and a restrained blue for links. The landing animation sits on the page itself; the beam on Architecture is set in a dark panel.

Open Unified B
Layout
Title bar with the breadcrumbs, contents tree with search at its head, the page, an outline rail on wide screens, and a footer.
Navigation
The tree keeps Datasheet's order, with Reference second: Start, Reference, Architecture, Tutorials, Guides, Tools, Project. Previous and next follow it. On a phone, previous and next sit at the foot of the page, and the page outline is a fold.
Type
The same three faces as Unified A: Charis SIL, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono.

  • Reference sits second in the tree, closest to the top for a returning engineer.
  • The landing page brings the reader routes nearest the first screen.

  • On paper, the landing animation is quieter than the Exploded view original you preferred.
  • The reading order puts Reference before Tutorials, so Next from Overview leads into the address map rather than into the first steps.
  • On a phone, previous and next are only at the foot of a long page.
  • The beam's labels are set small inside the drawing.

How the two were made

Claude built Unified A and GPT-6.1 Sol built Unified B, from the same brief and in separate worktrees, without seeing each other's work. Each then reviewed the other's design with screenshots and the source, and each accepted or rejected every point.

Changes made to Unified A after Sol's review

  • Keyboard focus stays inside the phone drawer while a search is open. Before the fix, Tab could escape into the page behind it.
  • The address-space figure became a fold: open on the overview, closed on region links.
  • Search stays pinned at the head of the tree, and the tree scrolls to show the current page.
  • The phone reading bar names the previous and next pages.
  • The landing headline and the layers sit side by side from about 1440 pixels. On a phone, each layer's labels stay beside its plate.
  • Adopted from Unified B: the beam beside the Architecture opening paragraph, “Industry partners” as the audience name, and a page title on locally generated peripheral documents.

Changes made to Unified B after Claude's review

  • The canonical landing headline, introduction and figure title are restored. The first version had rewritten the headline and added a “RISC-V · simulation · FPGA” label above it.
  • The tree and previous and next now follow one order.
  • The beam is drawn directly. The first version briefly rendered the landing page behind the scenes to copy it.
  • Breadcrumbs show implementation status, and they moved into the title bar.
  • Reader routes show every step, not only the first three.
  • The address-space figure marks the region in view.
  • Adopted from Unified A: the beam's layer and contract buttons, which show each part in the block diagram.

Where the two still differ

  • Tree order. Unified A orders the tree for reading: Tutorials and Architecture before Reference. Unified B keeps Datasheet's order, with Reference second, for returning engineers. Each design's previous and next follow its own tree.
  • Landing surface. Unified A keeps Exploded view's dark band. Unified B keeps the layers on the page surface, so the landing page reads as part of the documentation.
  • Phone reading controls. Unified A has a fixed bar with previous, contents and next. Unified B keeps them at the foot of the page.

Datasheet

Prism as a reference manual. A navy title band, a full contents tree, ruled tables and a memory-map figure, in the manner of a semiconductor reference manual.

Open Datasheet
Layout
Three panes: contents tree, page, and a rail with the page outline. Dense, 15px base.
Navigation
Every page is in the tree; guides fold by group. Find (/) matches pages, guide sections, registers and fields, and resolves an address such as 0x40020008.
Type
Barlow, after the DIN lettering of engineering drawings, with a semi-condensed cut for labels; IBM Plex Mono for addresses and code.
Technical content
Tables ruled with a header band. An address-space figure, drawn from the generated memory map, opens the landing page and follows the reference pages as a jump list.

Strengths

  • Fastest lookup of the three: one click to any page, one query to any register or address.
  • Shows the most per screen, which suits register tables and the manifest reference.
  • Reads as an engineering tool, in line with the plain copy the site already uses.
  • Closest in structure to the current site, so the least to relearn.

Weaknesses

  • Least guidance for a newcomer: the landing page is a reference sheet, not an introduction.
  • Small, narrow type is the least comfortable for long guides and for low-vision readers.
  • The tree and rail leave the simulators the least width unless the reader hides the tree.
  • The most conventional of the three as a first impression of the project.

Coursebook

Prism as a course text. A reading column on an engineering pad, an ordered outline with previous and next on every page, and progress that is remembered.

Open Coursebook
Layout
A book: course outline, a reading column of about 75 characters a line at 18px, and a margin with this page's sections.
Navigation
The site in one reading order, in units. Previous and next on every page, and a dock with both at the bottom of a phone. Pages you have opened are marked, per unit.
Type
Charis SIL for prose; Atkinson Hyperlegible, drawn to keep 1, l and I or 0 and O apart, for labels, tables and code.
Technical content
Tutorial stages become a checklist that is saved in the browser. The guide index lists every guide with its stated goal. The prism illustration is kept, on grid paper.

Strengths

  • The most comfortable long-form reading: larger type, shorter lines, generous leading.
  • A student always knows where they are, what comes next and what they have done.
  • Strongest on a phone for reading: previous, outline and next stay within reach.
  • Typefaces chosen for legibility of code-like text.

Weaknesses

  • Slowest for lookup: reference pages sit in the last unit, and the outline folds.
  • The reading order is an editorial decision this prototype made; it needs the owner's review.
  • Reads as teaching material, which may undersell the project to an industrial evaluator.
  • Progress lives in one browser only, and the dock takes a strip of a phone screen.

Exploded view

Prism as a platform site. Black type on white with the four contract colours as the only colour, sections in a top bar, and a landing page that opens on the SoC drawn apart into its layers.

Open Exploded view
Layout
No side rails. Pages are centred, and the simulators take the full window. A slim strip with the path and this page's sections stays in view.
Navigation
Five sections in a top bar; each opens a panel of its pages with one-line descriptions. All pages again in the foot. Search (/) as in Datasheet.
Type
Archivo, from normal width for reading to its expanded width for headings, after instrument faceplates; Commit Mono for code.
Technical content
The landing figure takes its layer names from the canonical illustration and links each layer to its pages. Tables are open, with rules only between rows.

Strengths

  • The strongest first impression, and the figure explains the layering before any text is read.
  • Gives the simulators the most room, about 220 to 270 pixels more than the other two at 1440.
  • Section panels show a whole area at once, such as all 26 guides in their groups.
  • Three reader routes sit directly under the landing figure.

Weaknesses

  • No list of sibling pages while reading: moving between guides means reopening a panel.
  • The page outline is behind a button, not in view.
  • Closest of the three to a product site, though the copy is unchanged and plain.
  • The landing figure is an illustration of the layers, not a drawing of the hardware.

Recommendation

Both unified designs meet the five choices and pass the same checks; they now differ in three judgement calls, listed under How the two were made. Choose by those: reading order and a stronger landing page favour Unified A; reference first and one quiet surface favour Unified B. Either can take the other's position on any of the three, since both run on the same shared runtime.

The recommendation made for the first three directions, before the unified designs, follows for reference.

Use Exploded view as the shell, and carry two things over from the others. Its full-width pages suit the simulators, which are the site's most distinctive content, and its landing page serves a first-time evaluator best.

  • From Coursebook: previous and next on guides and tutorials, and the tutorial checklist. They cover Exploded view's weakest point, moving between guides, and they are what students gain most from.
  • From Datasheet: the address-space figure on the address map. It is the quickest way to jump between regions.

If the site should stay a pure reference manual with no landing page to speak of, Datasheet alone is the safer choice. The three share one runtime, so these borrowings are small.
